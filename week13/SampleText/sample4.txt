The current microservice-based system has been experiencing increasing latency during peak usage periods. Preliminary analysis suggests that the issue may stem from inefficient load balancing, suboptimal caching strategies, or blocking behavior caused by legacy synchronous service calls that have not yet been migrated to asynchronous execution. Additionally, the database query optimizer appears to be producing inefficient execution plans for complex join operations, potentially due to outdated index statistics or missing composite indexes.

Root cause analysis is further complicated by fragmented observability infrastructure. Logging is distributed across multiple monitoring platforms, and several services do not consistently emit structured logs, making it difficult to correlate metrics, traces, and error events. Furthermore, recent schema changes were deployed incrementally without a comprehensive regression testing pipeline, increasing the likelihood of undetected performance regressions.

Compounding these issues, the CI/CD pipeline occasionally fails due to dependency version drift across environments, leading to non-reproducible builds and slower hotfix deployment. Overall, improvements are needed in system observability, test coverage, dependency management, and performance optimization to restore reliability and scalability.
