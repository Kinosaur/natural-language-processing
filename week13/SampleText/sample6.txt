the software platform has been growing fast over the last few years but the architecture was not really planned to scale properly. many components were added ad-hoc and some services depend on others in unclear ways. because of this, small updates sometimes break unrelated features and debugging becomes very time-consuming. developers also use different coding standards and documentation is incomplete or outdated.

performance issues are becoming more frequent especially when traffic spikes. caching is partially implemented but invalidation is inconsistent so stale data appears. some database queries are slow because indexes are missing or tables are too large. monitoring dashboards exist but alerts are noisy and often ignored. logs are spread across multiple systems making tracing root causes difficult.

the machine learning components have similar problems. training datasets are collected from multiple sources with inconsistent labeling. preprocessing steps are not well documented, and data leakage may occur between training and evaluation splits. experiments are difficult to reproduce since random seeds, hyperparameters, and environment versions are not tracked properly. as a result, reported model improvements may not be reliable.

security and compliance are also concerning. some API keys and secrets are stored in configuration files instead of secure vaults. access control policies grant broader permissions than necessary. vulnerability scanning is performed occasionally but findings are not always addressed promptly. backups exist but restoration procedures have not been fully tested, so disaster recovery readiness is uncertain.

overall, the organization recognizes the need for refactoring and modernization but progress is slow due to tight deadlines and limited resources. technical debt continues to accumulate, and without a structured improvement plan, the platform risks becoming increasingly unstable and difficult to maintain.
